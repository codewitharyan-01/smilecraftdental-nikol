Orthodontics

Straightening teeth, at fifteen or at fifty.

Both are common here. The mechanics do not change with age — what changes is how much time you have for appointments, and how visible you are willing for the hardware to be.

Book this treatment Ask a question

Crowded teeth are harder to clean, which is why they decay more. That is the clinical argument. The other argument — that you would rather not hide your teeth in photographs — is just as valid, and we will not pretend otherwise.

The options, honestly compared

Metal braces

Stainless steel brackets and wires. The most predictable option for complex cases, and usually the least expensive. Visible, and the wires can irritate for the first week or two. Modern self-ligating brackets need fewer visits and hold less plaque than the older tied design.

Ceramic braces

Tooth-coloured brackets. Less obvious from a distance, slightly more fragile, and a little more expensive. The wire is still metal, so “invisible” is not the right word.

Clear aligners

A series of removable trays, changed every week or two, that move teeth in small planned steps. Excellent for crowding, spacing and mild bite correction. Not magic: they only work while they are in your mouth, and they need to be in for 20–22 hours a day. Patients who take them out for meetings, meals and photographs do not finish on schedule.

What it fixes

  • Crowding and overlapping teeth.
  • Gaps and spacing.
  • Deep bites, open bites and crossbites.
  • Teeth that have drifted after an earlier extraction or a lost tooth.
  • Relapse after orthodontics as a teenager — very common in the thirties and forties.

Jaw discrepancies in a growing child are a different problem, sometimes needing functional appliances or a referral. If that is what we see, we will say so at the first visit.

How the treatment runs

  1. Records. Photographs, a radiograph, and a scan or impressions. This is what the plan is built on.
  2. The plan. Which option, how long, whether any teeth need extracting to make room, and the cost — in writing.
  3. Fitting. Brackets bonded and wire placed, or the first aligners delivered with instructions and a schedule.
  4. Reviews. Every four to eight weeks for braces; longer intervals for aligners, with remote photo check-ins in between.
  5. Debonding. Braces removed, teeth cleaned and polished, retainers fitted.

Retention is not optional

Teeth move back. They do it for years after treatment ends, and no appliance prevents it entirely. A fixed wire behind the front teeth, a removable retainer at night, or both — usually for the long term.

Patients who stop wearing retainers after six months are the reason we see the same case twice. It is the least glamorous part of orthodontics and the part that decides whether the money was well spent.

What drives the cost

The type of appliance, the complexity of the case, how long it runs, whether extractions or additional appliances are needed, and the retainers at the end. Aligners are priced by the number of stages required.

Payments are usually split across the treatment rather than paid up front, with a portion at each review. Ask at the consultation and we will set out the schedule.

Questions

Things people ask before booking.

Am I too old for braces?

No. Provided the gums and bone are healthy, adults are treated routinely here. The main difference is that adult treatment takes a little longer and retention matters more.

Metal or clear — which should I choose?

It depends on the case and on you. Complex bite correction is more predictable with fixed braces; mild to moderate crowding is well suited to aligners. We will show you both plans with timelines rather than pushing one.

How often are the appointments?

Every four to eight weeks for braces. Aligners need fewer in-person visits — often six to eight weeks — with photo check-ins in between.

Do braces hurt?

They are uncomfortable rather than painful. The first week and the day after each adjustment are the worst of it: tenderness, and a wire that rubs. Soft food and the wax we give you get most people through.

Will I need teeth removed?

Sometimes, where there is significant crowding and not enough space. It is decided at the planning stage, not halfway through, and you will know before treatment starts.
