Cosmetic dentistry

A smile that still looks like yours.

The worst cosmetic dentistry is the kind people notice. Smile design here starts from your face, your age and your other teeth — not from a photograph of someone else.

Book this treatment Ask a question

Smile design is not a single procedure. It is a plan that may involve whitening, composite bonding, ceramic veneers, crowns, or simply cleaning and leaving things alone. The value is in deciding which, and in what order.

What it can change

  • Colour — staining that cleaning will not shift, or a single dark tooth.
  • Shape — chipped edges, worn biting surfaces, teeth that are short or overly rounded.
  • Proportion — a tooth that is noticeably wider or narrower than its neighbours.
  • Spacing — small gaps closed with bonding or veneers, larger ones with orthodontics first.
  • Gum line — where the gums sit unevenly across the front teeth.

What it cannot do is fix a bite. If the problem is alignment, orthodontics comes first and cosmetics second — otherwise you put expensive ceramic on teeth that are in the wrong place.

Who it suits

Adults with healthy teeth and gums, who want a specific change and can describe it. Also people whose front teeth have been chipped or worn for years and who have stopped smiling in photographs because of it.

It is not the right starting point where there is active decay, untreated gum disease, or heavy grinding that has not been addressed. Ceramic is unforgiving of a clenching habit; a night guard is part of the plan, not an upsell.

The process

  1. Consultation and photographs. Close-up and full-face photographs, a shade record, and a conversation about what you actually want to change.
  2. A proposal. Which teeth, which material, how many visits, and the cost.
  3. Trial smile or mock-up. Composite or a digital mock-up placed on the teeth without drilling, so you can see the proposal in your own mouth — and change your mind before anything is permanent.
  4. Preparation. Minimal for veneers — often a fraction of a millimetre of enamel — and none at all for bonding.
  5. Fitting. Impressions or a scan go to the lab; temporaries in the meantime; the final restorations are tried, adjusted and bonded.

The honest part

Veneers are permanent. Enamel does not grow back, and the teeth will always need some form of restoration afterwards. That is why we push the trial smile stage and why we will happily talk you out of treatment if whitening and cleaning would achieve most of what you want for a fraction of the cost.

We also will not promise a result that looks untouched by dentistry. Some clinics do. Teeth that have been rebuilt in ceramic look like teeth that have been rebuilt in ceramic — the aim is that they look well, in proportion, and like they belong to your face.

What drives the cost

The number of teeth involved, whether the material is composite or laboratory ceramic, the complexity of the shade matching, and whether gum contouring or orthodontics is needed first.

Composite bonding is done directly in the chair and costs less per tooth; ceramic veneers are made by a technician, take longer, and last differently. We will give you both numbers.

Questions

Things people ask before booking.

Will veneers look fake?

They can, and that is usually a shade or proportion problem rather than a materials problem. We match to the adjacent teeth and to your age, and we use the trial smile so you can see the proposal before committing.

How much tooth is removed?

For ceramic veneers, typically 0.3–0.7 mm of enamel — sometimes none, where the teeth are already worn. Composite bonding requires no drilling at all in most cases.

How long do veneers last?

Many years with good hygiene and a night guard if you grind. Chipping is the usual failure, and it is usually repairable. Bonding wears faster and is easier to refresh.

Can I just get my teeth whitened instead?

Very often, yes. If the teeth are the right shape and the only issue is colour, whitening is cheaper, reversible and does not remove tooth structure. We will say so if that is the case.

Does it hurt?

Preparation is done under local anaesthesia. Some sensitivity for a few days afterwards is normal, particularly with whitening, and settles.
