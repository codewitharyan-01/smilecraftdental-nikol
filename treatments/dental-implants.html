Implant centre

Dental implants, planned before anything is drilled.

A titanium post takes the place of the root; a crown takes the place of the tooth. It is the closest thing dentistry has to putting a tooth back — and it only works if the planning is honest about the bone you have.

Book this treatment Ask a question

Most people arrive at this page because they are missing a tooth and have been told, or have read, that a bridge means grinding down the two healthy teeth either side. That is the single best argument for an implant, and it is a fair one.

What an implant actually is

Three parts. A titanium fixture that is placed into the jawbone and, over a few months, fuses with it. An abutment that screws into the fixture and connects to the visible tooth. And a crown — ceramic or zirconia — that is shaped and shaded to match its neighbours.

Because nothing is joined to the adjacent teeth, they stay untouched. Because the fixture sits in bone, it also keeps that bone from shrinking away the way it does after an extraction.

Who it suits — and who it doesn’t

Implants work well for a single missing tooth, for several missing teeth (sometimes on two implants rather than one each), and for a full arch, where four to six implants can carry a fixed bridge.

What matters is the bone. If there has been a tooth missing for years, or there has been gum disease, there may not be enough volume in the right place. That is not a dead end — a graft can usually rebuild it — but it adds a step and a few months, and you should be told that before you commit rather than after.

It is generally not the right answer where the bone is still growing, where uncontrolled diabetes or heavy smoking makes healing unpredictable, or where the underlying gum disease has not been treated. We will say so plainly if that is your situation.

The process, step by step

  1. Consultation and imaging. A clinical examination and a radiograph; for more complex cases, a CBCT scan to measure the bone in three dimensions.
  2. A written plan. How many implants, what length and diameter, whether a graft is needed, how long it will take, and what it costs. You take this away with you.
  3. Placement. Under local anaesthesia. The site is opened, the bone is prepared with sequential drills, and the fixture is placed. Most people describe pressure rather than pain. Sutures come out after seven to ten days.
  4. Healing. Three to six months, during which the bone integrates with the fixture. You will have a temporary tooth or a small healing cap in the meantime.
  5. The crown. An impression or scan, a shade match, and the final crown fitted — usually two short visits.

In selected cases — good bone, a single tooth, no infection — the implant and a temporary crown can be done in one stage. It is not suitable for everyone, and any clinic that offers it to everyone is not planning properly.

Afterwards

The first two or three days involve some swelling, and possibly a little bruising. Cold packs, the prescribed medication and soft food for a few days covers most of it. Avoid smoking for at least a fortnight — it is the single biggest avoidable cause of an implant failing.

Long term, an implant needs cleaning like a tooth, and rather more attention at the gum line, because it has no ligament to warn you when something is wrong. We will put you on a review schedule rather than simply waving you off.

What drives the cost

There is no single price for an implant, at this clinic or any other, because the price is really the sum of several decisions: how many implants, which system, whether a bone graft or sinus lift is needed, what the crown is made of, and whether a temporary restoration is required during healing. Two patients with “one missing tooth” can have very different plans.

We quote in writing after the examination, item by item, and we do not revise it upwards mid-treatment without telling you why. Ask us for the figure before you book, and ask the same question of any clinic you are comparing us with.

Questions

Things people ask before booking.

Does getting an implant hurt?

The placement itself is done under local anaesthesia, so you should not feel sharp pain — pressure and vibration, yes. Most people take a painkiller for a day or two afterwards and are back at work the next day.

How long does the whole thing take?

Typically four to six months from placement to final crown, because the bone needs time to integrate. If a graft is required, add three to four months before placement even begins. Single-stage cases can be faster.

Can I have an implant if I have diabetes?

Often yes, if it is well controlled and your physician is comfortable. Poorly controlled diabetes affects healing and we would want that addressed first. Bring your recent reports to the consultation.

What if there isn’t enough bone?

A bone graft — either at the time of extraction or later — can usually rebuild the site. It adds a procedure and a few months, and it is planned for, not discovered halfway through.

Is a bridge cheaper?

Usually, yes, in the short term. A bridge also means preparing the two adjacent teeth, which may be perfectly healthy. Over fifteen or twenty years the arithmetic often reverses. We will lay out both, honestly, and let you choose.
