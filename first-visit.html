Your first visit

What actually happens in the first forty minutes.

No surprises, no treatment on the first visit unless you are in pain and ask for it. This is what the appointment looks like.

The conversation

We start with questions, not instruments. What brought you in, how long it has been going on, what makes it better or worse, what you have already tried. If you have had treatment elsewhere, bring whatever was given to you — a plan, an X-ray, a prescription. It saves repeating work.

The examination

A visual check of every tooth and the gums, with a small mirror and a probe. We check the bite, the jaw joint and the soft tissue. Nothing is drilled at this stage.

If a radiograph is needed — and for most new patients one is — we take it and go through it with you on the screen, pointing at the actual thing we are talking about rather than describing it in the air.

The plan

You leave with it written down: what needs doing now, what can wait, what is optional, and what each part costs. If the honest answer is “come back in six months”, that is what the plan will say.

If you are in pain, we will treat the cause of the pain that day where it is possible. Everything else can be planned calmly afterwards.

What to bring

  • Any previous dental records, X-rays or treatment plans.
  • A list of medicines you take regularly, or the strip itself.
  • Recent blood sugar or blood pressure reports, if you have them.
  • For a child, their immunisation record is not needed — but knowing their age and whether they have been to a dentist before helps.

If you are nervous

Say so at the start, on WhatsApp if it is easier. It changes how the appointment is run: shorter, with breaks, and the chair kept upright the first time. You can stop the appointment at any point by raising your left hand — that is the signal, and we will stop.

If you are bringing a child

The first visit is a look and a chat, not a treatment. Ideally before anything hurts, so the chair and the light are not associated with pain. Bring them along to your own appointment if that helps — watching a parent be examined is the best preparation there is.

Paying

Cash, UPI and cards are accepted. Larger plans can be split across sittings. Ask for the written estimate before treatment begins; you are entitled to it and we would rather give it than have you guess.

At a glance

A consultation, an examination and a written plan. Roughly 30–45 minutes.
